# Pearson Edexcel
Pearson offers three Computer Science qualifications that are relevant here: a GCSE (1CP2), an International GCSE (4CP0), and a brand-new International A Level, first taught from September 2026. Pearson doesn't offer a UK A Level in Computer Science.

## GCSE (1CP2)
| Paper   | What it covers                            | Format                   | Length            | Marks | Weighting |
|---------|-------------------------------------------|--------------------------|-------------------|-------|-----------|
| Paper 2 | Application of computational thinking     | On-screen, in Python 3   | 2 hours           | 75    | 50%       |

Paper 1 (the other 50%) is a written exam on the principles of computer science.

- In Paper 2, you write, test and refine real Python 3 programs, using an IDE (see [Lesson 0](../lessons/00-getting-started)). You're given code files to work on, and a _Programming Language Subset_ (PLS): a document listing the parts of Python that the exam can ask you to use, on paper and on screen. Pearson won't set tasks that go beyond it, so get the latest version from Pearson's website and get to know it well.
- **Which lessons?** [Lessons 0 to 10](../README.md#fundamentals) cover the programming you need. The later lessons are extension material.

## International GCSE (4CP0)
The International GCSE is assessed differently to the GCSE, even though the papers have the same names.

| Paper   | What it covers                            | Format                          | Length  | Marks | Weighting |
|---------|-------------------------------------------|---------------------------------|---------|-------|-----------|
| Paper 1 | Principles of computer science            | Written                         | 2 hours | 80    | 50%       |
| Paper 2 | Application of computational thinking     | Part written, part on-screen    | 3 hours | 80    | 50%       |

- Paper 2 mixes written questions (answered on the question paper) with task-based questions, which you complete on a computer.
- You can program in **Python, C# or Java**.
- Pearson has its own pseudocode for the International GCSE, which is listed in an appendix of the specification. A pseudocode reference document is available during the exam, but make sure you're familiar with it beforehand.
- **Which lessons?** [Lessons 0 to 10](../README.md#fundamentals).

## International A Level (from 2026)
Pearson's International Advanced Level (IAL) in Computer Science is a completely new qualification: first teaching was in September 2026, with the first International AS exams in 2027. It has four units, each worth 25% of the full International A Level:

| Unit   | What it covers                                    | Format                               | Length             | Marks |
|--------|---------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------|--------------------|-------|
| Unit 1 | Principles of computer science                    | Written (on paper or on-screen)      | 1 hour 30 minutes  | 80    |
| Unit 2 | Practical programming and problem-solving         | On-screen, in Python 3               | 3 hours            | 80    |
| Unit 3 | Advanced principles of computer science           | Written (on paper or on-screen)      | 1 hour 30 minutes  | 80    |
| Unit 4 | Advanced practical programming and problem-solving | On-screen, in Python 3              | 3 hours            | 80    |

Units 1 and 2 make up the International AS Level, and Units 3 and 4 complete the International A Level. As with the GCSE, the practical units are taken in an IDE, with a digital Programming Language Subset (PLS) document for reference.
